Weight is one of the hardest things to discuss in a short appointment, in both directions. Patients often leave with advice too general to act on. Clinicians often have less time than the subject needs.

This page will not tell you how to lose weight. It will help you get a specific plan from someone qualified to give you one.

## Decide what you actually want from the conversation

These are different requests, and they lead to different appointments:

- **Information.** What does my weight mean for my health specifically, given my history?
- **Investigation.** Could something be contributing - thyroid, medications, sleep, a hormonal cause?
- **A plan.** What is a realistic approach for me, and what support exists?
- **A referral.** To a dietitian, a weight management program, or a specialist.
- **Options.** What treatments exist, what they involve, and what I would qualify for.

Say which one you want at the start. "I would like a referral to a dietitian" gets a different response than "I need to lose weight."

## Ask for a longer appointment

This is the most useful practical step and almost nobody takes it. When booking, say the visit is to discuss weight and ask for a longer slot. Many practices have them. A subject this large does not fit into the tail end of a visit about something else.

## Questions worth asking

**About your own situation**

- Given my history and my labs, what specifically concerns you about my weight, if anything?
- Are any of my current medications known to make weight loss harder?
- Are there any conditions worth ruling out before we treat this as purely lifestyle?

**About a plan**

- What would a realistic goal look like for me over the next six months?
- What is the smallest change you would start with?
- How will we measure whether this is working, and when do we reassess?

**About support**

- Does my insurance cover a dietitian, and how many visits?
- Is there a weight management program you refer people to?
- What options exist beyond diet and exercise, and would I be a candidate?

## If the conversation does not go well

Some appointments about weight go badly. If you leave feeling dismissed, or if every symptom you raise is attributed to your weight without examination, that is a legitimate reason to seek a [second opinion](/tips/getting-a-second-opinion/).

It is also reasonable to say so in the room: "I would like this symptom looked into on its own terms as well." Clinicians generally respond to a direct, specific request.

## What we are not going to do here

We are not going to publish a diet, a calorie target, or an exercise plan. Not because the topic is off limits, but because the useful version of that advice depends on your medical history, your medications, your joints, your schedule, and a physical examination - and a website has none of that.

What we can do is help you get to someone who does have it, prepared. If you want to record what you eat and how you move before a dietitian appointment, the [food and activity log](/tools/trackers/) is a blank sheet for it, and dietitians almost always ask for one.

If you arrived here from our [BMI calculator](/tools/bmi/), it is worth repeating what that page says: BMI is a ratio of weight to height and nothing else. It cannot tell muscle from fat and it was never designed to assess an individual. It is a prompt for a conversation, not the conversation.
