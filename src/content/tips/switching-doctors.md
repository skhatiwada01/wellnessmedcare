You can leave a doctor's practice the way you leave a grocery store. No permission, no exit interview, no reason on file. The only real work in switching is moving your records and not running out of refills in the gap. Both are manageable if you sequence them.

## Your records are yours by law

HIPAA gives you the right to a copy of your medical records. The practice cannot refuse because you're leaving, because of an unpaid balance, or because "the doctor has to approve it." They can charge a reasonable copying fee, and they generally must deliver within 30 days.

Two ways to move the records:

1. You request a copy for yourself, in writing - the letter template is in [templates](/templates/#medical-records). Ask for electronic copies; they're cheaper, often free, and you keep a set forever.
2. The new practice pulls them. Sign a release form at the new office and they request the records directly. Less work for you, but you never see the file, and files get sent incomplete.

Doing both is not paranoid. Either way, ask for the full record: notes, labs, imaging reports, immunizations, and the medication list. "Visit summaries" alone are the trailer, not the movie.

## Time the switch around your refills

The gap between doctors is where prescriptions fail. New-patient appointments can be booked out weeks or months, and many practices will not refill for someone they have not seen. So sequence it:

1. Count what you have. Check each prescription for remaining refills and days on hand.
2. Book the new-patient appointment before anything else. The wait time defines your whole timeline.
3. If a prescription runs out before that appointment, call your current office and ask about refills to bridge the gap. You are still their patient until you are not, and refill requests are routine.
4. Put your medication list in order before the first visit - the printable list in the [trackers](/tools/trackers/) is built for it.

## You owe the old practice nothing

No explanation required. "Please send my records to [new practice]" is a complete sentence. If you want to say something, one line does it - "we're switching practices" - and nobody will interrogate you. Awkwardness is not a fee you have to pay.

Separate issue: if you are leaving because something went wrong and you want it addressed, that is a complaint process - the practice manager, your insurer, or the state medical board - and it runs independently of your switch. Leaving and complaining are both allowed. Neither requires the other.

## Land well at the new practice

Confirm the new doctor is in your network before the first visit. Directories go stale, so verify with both the office and your insurer - [in-network vs out-of-network](/guides/in-network-vs-out-of-network/) shows how. Then treat the first visit as the setup investment it is: records transferred and confirmed received, medication list in hand, history organized. [First appointment with a new doctor](/tips/first-appointment-new-doctor/) covers that visit start to finish.

The sequence, so nothing falls in the gap:

1. Book the new-patient appointment first.
2. Request records twice - your own copy and the practice-to-practice transfer.
3. Bridge any refills that expire before the new appointment.
4. Confirm the new practice actually received the records before you arrive.
5. Tell the old practice as much or as little as you like. Nothing is fine.
