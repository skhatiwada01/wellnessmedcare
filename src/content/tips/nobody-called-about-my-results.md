"No news is good news" describes how offices behave, not how systems work. Results get faxed to the wrong number, filed without review, attached to the wrong chart, or seen and never communicated. From your side, a normal result and a lost one look identical: silence. The only way to tell them apart is to ask.

## Silence has boring causes

The failure points are mundane. The lab sent the result to the ordering doctor's old fax line. The result posted to the chart while the doctor was on vacation and the covering doctor's queue swallowed it. The office policy is "we only call about abnormal results" and yours was fine - or the policy is that, the result was not fine, and the call fell through anyway. None of this is rare. Studies of missed results exist because the problem is common enough to study.

## Check the portal first

Most results now release to your patient portal as soon as they are final, often before anyone calls. Log in and look before you pick up the phone. If the result is there, [understanding your lab report](/tips/understanding-your-lab-report/) explains what the flags and reference ranges mean, and the [questions sheet builder](/understand/my-lab-results/) turns the numbers into questions for the follow-up conversation. A result in the portal still deserves that conversation - what it means for you is your clinician's call, not the flag column's.

## The follow-up script

If the portal has nothing, call the office that ordered the test:

> "I had [test] on [date], ordered by Dr. [name]. I haven't received the result. Can you tell me whether it's in my chart, whether the doctor has reviewed it, and what the next step is?"

Three parts, deliberately: in the chart, reviewed, next step. "It's here" is not the same as "someone looked at it."

## The escalation ladder

If one call does not resolve it, climb:

1. Call the office again and ask for a callback deadline: "By when will someone call me back? I'll call again after that."
2. Call the lab or imaging center directly. Ask whether the result was finalized and where it was sent. Now you know which end lost it.
3. Send a portal message with the same script, so there is a written record.
4. Request the report itself through a records request - the template is in [templates](/templates/#medical-records). You are entitled to it.
5. Ask for the office manager. Missing results are a patient-safety issue, and office managers treat them as one.

Keep a log of dates and names as you go. The appointment notes sheets in the [trackers](/tools/trackers/) work for this.

## Close the loop before the next test

The fix for next time takes ten seconds at the moment of the test. Before you leave, ask:

> "How will I get this result, when should I expect it, and who do I call if I haven't heard by then?"

Write down the answer. If they say "we'll call within a week," the calendar entry on day eight says "call about result." More prep-side detail is in [preparing for a lab test](/tips/preparing-for-a-lab-test/).

The rule that replaces "no news is good news":

1. Every test gets a result date and an owner before you leave the building.
2. Silence past that date triggers your call, not your patience.
3. Every result - normal or not - is confirmed as reviewed, not just received.
